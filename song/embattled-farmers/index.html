Embattled Farmers

bfo.mantis

More about this track

Embattled Farmers starts with a midnight alarm on April 19, 1775. Revere cuts through the dark, plows are left in the furrow, and seventy Minutemen step onto the green while Parker tells them not to fire first. Eight men fall in the dew before the farmers regroup at the North Bridge. Wives watch from windows, a father whispers to his daughter at the door, and every free breath is counted as paid for there.

Lyrics

April 19, 1775… midnight alarm. No cap. Farmers woke up kings. Liberty or death. God first, then the clip. Let’s ride. Midnight hoofbeats thunder, Revere cuttin’ through the dark, Lanterns in the steeple—two if by sea, we spark. Plows left in the furrow, wives clutchin’ babies tight, These ain’t soldiers, just men who knew wrong from right. Concord powder stash, British boots comin’ for the steel, Seventy Minutemen step to the green, hearts of chilled steel. Parker stands tall, voice steady through the fear: “Don’t fire first, boys—but if they want it, make it clear.” Redcoats in formation, bayonets catch the dawn, Farmers in homespun, muskets older than their sons. First shot cracks the silence—nobody knows who lit the fuse, But that spark lit a fire no empire could ever use. Embattled farmers! Standin’ on God’s sovereign ground, Liberty above all but the throne where the King is crowned. Blood on the North Bridge, smoke in the April breeze, We fired the shot heard round the world—now the tyrants on their knees! No king, no crown, no chain gon’ break these hands, We die for the free, leave the farm for the free man’s stand. Sovereignty eternal, etched in every grave we fill— God, then liberty, or we die where we build! Lexington Green turned red, brothers droppin’ in the dew, Eight good men gone before the sun even knew. But we fell back swingin’, regrouped at the rude bridge arch, Barrett, Buttrick, Davis—Acton boys lead the charge. Smoke from the town, they thought we burnin’ our own, Four hundred farmers marched like the devil’s own storm. British volley hit first—then we answered with hell, Isaac Davis dropped, but his men still gave ’em the swell. Redcoats break and run, militia harrowin’ the road, Every rock, every tree, every farm boy unload. Wives watch from windows, tears mixin’ with pride, Knowin’ if they don’t come home, at least their sons ain’t gonna hide. Picture the sacrifice—last kiss at the door at 2 a.m., Man whispers to his daughter, “Daddy fightin’ for your amen.” Fields he tilled for years now soaked in patriot red, Mothers bury husbands while the church bells still bled. They wasn’t seekin’ glory, wasn’t chasin’ no throne, Just ordinary men who refused to bow to the unknown. No more stamps, no more tea, no more chains on the free, God gave us these rights—kings don’t get to decree. From the bridge to Bunker Hill, from the blood to the pen, They planted the seed of a nation that’ll never bend again. Every scar on that road, every unmarked grave in the grass, Screams: sovereignty first—after God, we never ask. We the embattled. We the unheard. We the spark. Farmers turned gangsters when the empire went too dark. No retreat, no surrender, no compromise on the soul— Liberty in our veins, sovereignty carved in stone. Above all but the Father who breathed life in our chest, We stood on that green so the future could rest. Embattled farmers! Echo through the centuries loud, Liberty sovereign—God’s gift, never bowed! Shot heard round the world still ringin’ in the air, Every free breath we take? They paid for it there. Rest in power, you legends in homespun and grit— We ride for the same flag. We never gon’ quit. April 19… forever. Embattled farmers. Gangsters for God and the free. The end of kings. The birth of we.
