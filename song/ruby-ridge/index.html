Ruby Ridge

bfo.mantis

More about this track

An unsparing spoken account of the August 1992 siege at Ruby Ridge, Idaho, told squarely from the Weaver family's side. It recounts the deaths of Sammy and Vicki Weaver in graphic detail and is fiercely critical of the federal response.

Lyrics

Yo, listen up — this ain’t no history book fairy tale. This is the raw, uncut slaughter at Ruby Ridge, where the feds turned a hillbilly with a bad court date into public enemy number one and painted the Idaho mountains red with Weaver blood. Randy Weaver? Just a man who wanted to be left the fuck alone on his 20-acre slice of nowhere. No electricity, no running water, raising his kids off-grid with his wife Vicki. They had their beliefs — weird ones, sure — but last time I checked, thinking the world’s ending don’t give the government a license to play judge, jury, and executioner with .308 rounds. First they send in the ATF with their snitch games. “Sell us some illegal shotgun barrels, Randy.” Classic entrapment hustle. Weaver bites once, they slap him with a bullshit warrant. Then they fuck up the court date on purpose — or “accidentally,” depending on which liar you believe — and when he don’t show, boom: full fugitive status. U.S. Marshals roll up like it’s the goddamn invasion of Normandy, six deep, armed to the teeth, crawling through the woods like cockroaches in tactical gear. August 21, 1992. The family dog, Striker, does what any loyal dog does — barks at the intruders. So these federal heroes light the dog up first. Sammy Weaver, fourteen years old, sees his dog murdered and opens fire. Next thing you know, Deputy Marshal Bill Degan is dead, Sammy’s shot in the back while running for his life, and Kevin Harris is bleeding out. One kid, one dog, one fed — all in the first five minutes of “law enforcement.” Day two? The FBI Hostage Rescue Team rolls in like they’re storming Baghdad. They write themselves brand-new “rules of engagement” on the spot — basically a shoot-on-sight pass for any armed adult male. No negotiation, no surrender, just “if they got a gun, light ‘em up.” That ain’t policy, that’s a hunting license with a government stamp. Sniper Lon Horiuchi sets up 200 yards out. Randy steps out toward the shed where his dead son is lying. First shot rips through Randy’s arm. Then Vicki Weaver — unarmed, holding her ten-month-old baby Elisheba in the cabin doorway — steps into view for half a second. Horiuchi pulls the trigger again. The bullet goes through the door, blows Vicki’s head apart, and shreds Kevin Harris too. A mother holding her infant. Executed in her own home. The baby was covered in her mother’s brains. And the feds? They left Vicki’s body rotting in the doorway for days while they kept the siege going. No apology. No “oops.” Just more lies, more spin, more “we feared for our lives” while they had night-vision, helicopters, and enough firepower to level a city block. The trial? A goddamn joke. Jury looked at the evidence and basically said, “Y’all federal boys murdered a kid and a mom over two shotgun barrels and a missed court date.” Weaver and Harris walked on the heavy charges. Government had to cough up $3.1 million in hush money to the family — blood money, not justice. This wasn’t law enforcement. This was a government death squad playing cowboy on American soil. They shot the dog for barking. They shot the kid in the back. They headshot a mother holding a baby. Then they spent years covering their asses, destroying reports, and giving each other pats on the back. Ruby Ridge wasn’t a “tragedy.” It was a slaughter. A cold-blooded flex by the same machine that would go on to Waco, and then wonder why the whole country started looking at federal badges like they were gang colors. They didn’t come for Randy Weaver because he was dangerous. They came because he told them no. And when the feds hear “no,” they don’t send lawyers. They send snipers. Rest in power to Sammy and Vicki. The rest of those federal clowns can rot in the hell they earned.
