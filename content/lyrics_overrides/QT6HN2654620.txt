They may torture my body,
break my bones,
even kill me…
Then they will have my dead body—
not my obedience.

Frail frame, spinning wheel, soft voice like a blade
Empire of iron, five hundred years of trade
They mapped the world in blood and called it law
He walked barefoot and rewrote the whole damn score
Salt in his hand, the ocean in his eyes
One man against the crown that never compromised
They said “Impossible”—he answered with his breath
“Break the body if you must… you still won’t own what’s left.”

Chains can hold the flesh,
guns can stop the heart,
but the will that answers only to the truth
cannot be torn apart.

They may torture my body, break my bones, even kill me—
Then they will have my dead body, not my obedience.
You can burn the house, scatter the ashes to the wind,
but the fire that refused to kneel still rises from within.
One man. One truth. One unbreakable spine.
The empire learned the hard way:
you cannot cage the divine.

Nightsticks cracked ribs, prisons swallowed years,
hunger strikes that made the strongest men disappear
They called it order. He called it what it was—
Tyranny dressed in uniforms and foreign laws.
He never raised a fist, yet armies had to yield,
non-violence sharper than any battlefield.
They measured power in steel and stolen gold,
he measured it in the courage of the controlled.
Impossible? Watch the map of India bleed free—
one soft-spoken storm dismantled the machinery.

You can silence the mouth,
you can bury the name,
but the soul that answers only to the light
will set the dark aflame.

They may torture my body, break my bones, even kill me—
Then they will have my dead body, not my obedience.
You can burn the house, scatter the ashes to the wind,
but the fire that refused to kneel still rises from within.
One man. One truth. One unbreakable spine.
The empire learned the hard way:
you cannot cage the divine.

They had the guns.
They had the gold.
They had the gallows and the cold.
He had nothing but a will
that would not fold.
And that was enough
to make the world remember
what it means to be free.

They may torture my body, break my bones, even kill me—
Then they will have my dead body, not my obedience.
You can burn the house, scatter the ashes to the wind,
but the fire that refused to kneel still rises from within.
One man. One truth. One unbreakable spine.
History still whispers his name
when tyrants try to claim
what never belonged to them.

Dead body…
not my obedience.
Not now.
Not ever.
