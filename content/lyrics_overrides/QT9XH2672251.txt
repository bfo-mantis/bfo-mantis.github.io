Marbury v. Madison, eighteen-oh-three.
A landmark decision more than two centuries old.
In a unanimous opinion by Chief Justice John Marshall, delivered February twenty-fourth,
the Court held for the first time that an act of Congress was unconstitutional—and therefore void.
Under Marbury, any law or rule that conflicts with the Constitution is void.
It is not law at all.
Marshall wrote: “A legislative act contrary to the Constitution is not law.”
Courts must give the Constitution effect over every conflicting ordinary rule or statute.
