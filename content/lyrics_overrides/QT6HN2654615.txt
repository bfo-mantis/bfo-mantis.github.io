Saigon bled in nineteen-sixty-three,
Diệm’s Catholic crown crushed the majority.
Pagodas stormed at midnight, blood on marble floors,
Buddhist flags ripped down like they were crimes of war.
Mothers hid their children while the gunfire sang,
Temples turned to prisons for the crime of being “wrong.”
A whole nation bowed its head beneath the cross they never chose—
Till one man stood up tall… and quietly said “no.”
No army, no army behind him, just robes and gasoline,
Yet every tyrant on Earth felt the tremor in his veins.
He sat in lotus calm amid the gathering crowd,
Poured the fire upon himself, head unbowed.
One match struck in silence, flame rose slow and sure—
Ten minutes upright in the blaze, heart enduring pure.
Flesh turned to ash, yet his form held the line,
A pillar of stillness in the inferno’s design.
One whisper… one match… one unyielding flame—
And the weight of oppression began to break its name.
They banned the lotus, they banned the prayer,
They killed the young, they killed the elder there.
But in his eyes there burned a light no bullet could erase,
A sovereign heart that chose the fire to give his people grace.
While the world watched in horror, cameras clicked and cried,
He sat perfectly still as the flames ate him alive.
No scream escaped his lips, no tear blurred his sight—
Just perfect, terrible peace in the heart of that light.

Peaceful—yet never harmless, never broken, never gone,
A quiet roar of virtue that echoes on and on.
One whisper… one match… one unyielding flame—
And the chains of a nation bowed beneath his name.
What kind of love would walk into hell with open arms?
What kind of courage sets its own flesh as the alarm?
He wasn’t fighting for power, he wasn’t fighting for fame—
He burned so the voiceless would never burn in vain.
From the ashes of one man rose a thousand burning hearts,
Kennedy read the wires… and the regime fell apart.
With a whisper and a match he lit the eternal flame,
Defiance burning deeper than any war could claim.
One man, one act, one match, one flame—
Thích Quảng Đức—your silence still calls our name.
Ten minutes strong, ten minutes free,
You gave your life so we could see…
That a silence no gun could ever silence,
A heart no fire could ever tame—
The peaceful, untamed heart…
Remains the most enduring flame.
One quiet act…
One unbreakable will…
Shifted history…
And the flame is burning… still.
