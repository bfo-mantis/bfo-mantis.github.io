WE ARE DANGEROUS!

You keep the lights on so we never see
who owns the switch and who bleeds the fee.
You write the law in other men’s blood
then wipe the wood and call it good.
You stamp our names like warehouse stock—
pulse on a tag, price on the clock.
Every cage is built the same:
it only works if we stay tame.
You fear the square before it fills.
You fear the mouth that won’t stay still.
You fear the street that learned the lock.
Your whole machine. Your holy state.
It runs on us.
We can unmake.

WE CAN UNMAKE!

Count us twice. You’re still short.

WE ARE DANGEROUS!
Your power was the fear we carried..
We kept your hours. We kept your lights.
We kept the secrets in the pipes.
WE ARE DANGEROUS!
Stop kneeling.
Watch what disappears.

You burned the books. The ash still maps
the alleys, blind spots, camera traps.
You numbered us to make us small.
The numbers know who doesn’t crawl.
You watch the roofs. We watch who’s gone.
You call it order. It’s a con.
Down here it’s theft in better clothes.
A ration line. A broken oath.—

—A tyrant is a man who knows
his reign is rented when eyes are closed —
from hands that clock, from mouths they shut,
from streets that swallow what he does.
Your strength was only fear of hurt.
You numbered names. You missed the fuse.
The people are the thing you use.
Fear was your only real police..
You need our backs to wear that skin.
Without our fear you are a myth.
Without our hands its just a chair.
Without our silence—nothing there.

Now fear the men who Will Not Kneel.

WE ARE DANGEROUS!
Not noise. The working cost.
WE ARE DANGEROUS!
