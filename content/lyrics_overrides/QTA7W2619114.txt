Sixteen thirty-eight, a cart-tail through the London mud,
Fleet to Westminster, every yard paid down in blood.
They stood him in the pillory; he preached it to the square.
They gagged him, and his pamphlets kept on flying through the air.
They wanted him to swear the oath and testify against himself —
Freeborn John said "No," and that No became the law itself.

Every crown is built on a million nods.
Every crown comes down on a single No.

Freeborn! Nobody gave it, nobody takes it!
Freeborn! No crown can fake it, no court can break it!
It came in the lungs with your very first cry —
one man says No, and the old world dies!

Worms, fifteen twenty-one, a monk before the crown,
the Emperor and the princes waiting for him to back down.
They piled his books upon the table: "Take it back. Recant."
He asked one night to sleep on it, then: "Here I stand. I can do no other."
Maryland, eighteen thirty-four, a breaker with a whip,
sixteen-year-old Douglass took him by the throat and would not lose his grip.
He wrote it after, plain as iron, so the world would understand:
"You've seen how a man was made a slave — now see a slave made man."

Montgomery, fifty-five, December, Cleveland Avenue line —
the driver said "Get up," and Rosa Parks said "No," and kept her seat.
One woman on one seat, and a city walked a year on its feet.
Prague, seventy-seven, Havel signs the Charter, and he will not go;
they cage him four years for saying it — and twelve years on, the square says No:
Wenceslas, November, keys held high and shaking in the cold,
the sound of every swallowed No, let out, a hundred thousandfold.
