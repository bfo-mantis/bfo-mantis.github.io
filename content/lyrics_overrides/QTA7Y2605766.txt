They never come with a fist.
They come with a favor.
They never say take.
They say let us keep you safe.

Juvenal laughed at bread and games, and still the crowds would roar,
while emperors wrote the quiet laws that freemen never saw before.
England hanged the witch for order, locked the parish under care,
then taxed the widow's hearthstone "for the good of all who share."
Wartime papers stamped Protection, stamped For You, stamped Keep Us Whole —
and a free man's name went quiet in a file they wouldn't show.

Help arrives with open hands.
Safety arrives with open plans.
Both of them ask for the keys.

For your own good —
that's the velvet on the blade.
For your own good —
they smile while the deal is made.
Give the keys for quieter nights,
find the quiet bought you none —
for your own good —
you're already undone.

Hard years made the builders: blistered palms and sleepless eyes,
they raised the roof, they dug the well, they taught their sons to rise.
Then the table filled, the fear went soft, the watchman took a chair,
and the sons said "keep us safe" to anyone who sounded fair.
They handed over powder, papers, wages, lands, and names —
and called it progress when the door swung shut behind the frames.

Prohibition was a kindness pouring poison down the drain —
until the kindness grew a badge and a boot and a chain.
Wartime camps were safety, signed in ink by shaking hands —
until the wire and the watchtowers taught them what that safety meant.
Emergency, then temporary, then forever with a smile —
every soft word is a hitch until the rope becomes a mile.

Hard times forge.
Good times fatten.
Comfort yields the lock.
Dark walks in wearing help —
then the storm breaks the clock.
Out of the fire, the hard ones rise.
Hard times forge again.
