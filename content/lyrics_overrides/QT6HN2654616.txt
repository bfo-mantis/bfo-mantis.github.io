Lynchburg boy with a Bible in his hand,
Seventh-day Adventist, no gun in the plan.
They called him coward, yellow belly, weak,
Told him to leave the fight, go hide and seek.
But he stood in the line, medic patch on his sleeve,
Swore to heal, not kill— that's what he'd believe.
Through Guam and Leyte, the fire never broke him,
Okinawa called, and Hacksaw Ridge spoke grim.
Four hundred feet of jagged rock and death,
Machine guns chatterin', stealin' every breath.
Battalion charged, then shattered, fell back in the smoke,
Left the wounded screamin', beggin' for a rope.
Lord, help me get one more!
One more soul from the fire, one more through the door.
No rifle, no blade, just a prayer and a cord,
Lowerin' brothers down where the angels afford.
Seventy-five miracles in a single night,
Desmond Doss on the ridge, turnin' wrong into right.
While the world was killin', he was savin' the fight—
One more... Lord, help me get one more!
Artillery rainin', mortars tearin' the ground,
Seventy-five hit, scattered bodies all around.
Captain ordered retreat, but Doss wouldn't run,
Stayed in the kill zone till the work was done.
Dragged 'em through the bullets, tied that double bowline knot,
Three hundred fifty feet to the bottom he brought.
One by one, swingin' slow like a pendulum swing,
Prayin' out loud while the devil pulled strings.
They mocked him for his Sabbath, for refusin' the steel,
But when the smoke cleared, they knew what was real.
He crawled to the cave mouth, grenades burstin' near,
Dressed their wounds in the face of the fear.
Pulled a man from the wire, carried him a hundred yards,
Never once raised a hand to harm.
Even tried for the enemy, saw the human inside,
'Cause his war was mercy, not genocide.
Lord, help me get one more!
Through the storm and the roar, through the blood on the floor.
No hate in his heart, just a light in the war,
Seventy-five times he answered the call.
From the edge of the cliff to the hands down below,
He lowered 'em gentle where the safe waters flow.
Hacksaw Ridge hero, the quietest roar—
One more... yeah, Lord, help me get one more!
Truman pinned the medal, said "I'm proud of you, son,"
"I consider this greater than bein' number one."
First conscientious objector to wear that gold star,
Proved you can fight hell without goin' to war.
He went home to Virginia, lived humble and true,
But the ridge still echoes what one man can do.
Wherever the battle, wherever the pain,
Some carry rifles, some carry the flame.
Desmond Doss showed us the bravest of all—
Hands that heal, not harm, answerin' the call.
Lord... help us get one more.
One more... one more...
