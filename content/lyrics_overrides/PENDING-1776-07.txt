[Verse 1]
In the halls of independence, our forefathers cried,
"Give me liberty or give me death," with fire in their eyes.
They shook off the chains of tyrants, across the ocean wide,
Jefferson warned, "The tree of liberty must be refreshed,"
With the blood of patriots and tyrants, in the fight for what's blessed.
Washington preserved the sacred fire, Adams hoped we'd cherish,
The cost of freedom they paid dearly, so our dreams wouldn't perish.
[Chorus]
Awake! Awake, ye patriots bold! The tyrants' chains draw near!
Freedom's torch is burning low—rise up, cast out the fear!
Stand! Stand! For liberty we fight, no more the tyrants' thrall!
Vote! Speak! Defend the sacred right—or watch the republic fall!
No slumber now, no compromise, the hour demands our all!
Reclaim! Restore! Renew the flame—before the freedoms call!
[Verse 2]
From Bunker Hill to Gettysburg, brave souls laid down their lives,
In the mud of trenches, on foreign shores, where sacrifice survives.
Freedom isn't free, it's bought with valor, in wars both just and grim,
Echoing Franklin's wisdom: Trade liberty for safety, and both grow dim.
They fought the king's oppression, rebelled against the crown,
Now we honor those who fell, in fields where heroes are found.
Madison foresaw the guise of foes, tyranny masked as defense,
Yet we stand on their shoulders, rebuking every pretense.

[Verse 3]
But history whispers warnings, of freedoms slowly lost,
The Alien Acts silenced voices, at liberty's great cost.
In the Nadir's dark shadow, sundown towns bred fear and hate,
Internment camps in wartime, sealed a shameful fate.
After towers fell, the Patriot's net ensnared the free,
Eroding rights in silence, under guise of security.
From Reconstruction's broken dreams to surveillance's cold embrace,
We must heed the turning points, or lose this sacred space.
[Bridge]
We built a constitutional republic, not mob rule or king's decree,
Where power flows from the people, yet bound by law eternally.
Elected guardians serve, but never reign unchecked and free—
A government of laws, not men, as Adams decreed.
Yet Franklin warned as he stepped into the light of day,
When asked what gift they'd wrought, he turned and boldly said:
"A republic—if you can keep it." The burden falls on thee!
