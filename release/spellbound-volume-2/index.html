Spellbound Volume 2

bfo.mantis

Spellbound Volume 2 trades the first volume's language of enchantment for the passing of time. Its titles watch a day burn down, from Morning Dew and Sun Down to Nightfall and Midnight Rain, while Sand of Time, Stolen Time and Endless Moments count what is slipping away and Firelight Embers and Embers follow a fire as it dies, all of it set in motion by an opening track called Fading. The sound moves between two worlds: slow, hazy late-night lo-fi built on distant piano, warm synth pads, vinyl crackle and a single ringing twangy note like a last ember, and fingerpicked acoustic country ballads with rolling thunder and soft rain behind them. Later tracks add light electric blues licks and muted ambient synths, so the storm and the fireside end up sharing the same long night.

Tracklist

  1. 1
    Fading Instrumental

    The album opens with something already slipping away. As the first title, it sets up a record about time passing, where things dim before they are fully seen.

    More about this track

    The album opens in late-night haze, with a lonely piano setting a minor-key mood before a gentle pulse, shimmering guitars, hazy synth pads and subtle cello gather around it. Faint wind and rain move in the background, and melodies drift up only to dissolve again. The slow, seamless long-form flow never holds onto anything for long, which suits a title about things slipping out of sight.

  2. 2
    Firelight Embers Instrumental

    The glow left in the hearth once the flames have settled. Early in the album, it sets a warm, quiet scene for the night ahead.

    More about this track

    One twangy note rings out and fades again and again, like a glowing coal that refuses to go out, and everything else settles around it. Distant piano, hazy synth pads and slow brushed beats drift through vinyl crackle and faint sounds of fire and wind. The long-form mood is cozy and melancholy at once, a room lit only by what is left in the hearth.

  3. 3
    Soft Wind's Instrumental

    A gentle breeze, the kind that stirs a fire rather than putting it out. Third on the album, it adds air and movement to the opening's warm stillness.

    More about this track

    Here the same ringing note and soft lo-fi bed feel less like a fire and more like air moving through a quiet house. Faint wind sounds stir behind hazy pads and a distant piano, while small reversed echoes and soft swells come and go like gusts. Slow and hypnotic over its long-form stretch, it keeps everything gentle, a breeze that never turns into weather.

  4. 4
    Embers Instrumental

    The fire again, burned lower now, with only the coals left. Two tracks after Firelight Embers, the title shows how far the night has already gone.

    More about this track

    Embers centers on the heart of this sound: a lone twangy note and a slow, steady pulse, with piano and warm pads moving softly around them. Tiny changes in how the note decays, like coals brightening and dimming, keep the long-form drift alive beneath the vinyl crackle. It is the album's quietest kind of warmth, heat that lingers after the light is gone.

  5. 5
    Sand of Time Instrumental

    Grains running through an hourglass, time made visible as it drains away. Near the center of the album, it names the theme the other titles keep circling.

    More about this track

    The ringing note becomes a kind of clock, each pluck decaying slowly like grains falling through glass. Around it, soft melodies wind through hazy synths, distant piano and delicate brushed beats at a very slow pace, with faint harmonic blooms and whispered swells marking the time. The seamless long-form flow makes the hours feel both endless and gone, just as the title suggests.

  6. 6
    Painting Clouds Instrumental

    A daydream title: someone lying back and shaping the sky into pictures. In the middle of the album, it offers an open-air pause from the night and the passing hours.

    More about this track

    The album steps outside into the acoustic country ballad sound that opened Volume 1: steady fingerpicked guitar and light percussion, with pedal steel and harmonica following the mood. Soft rain and rolling thunder sit in the background and build with each dramatic peak. With weather passing overhead, it fits a title about lying back and watching the sky change shape.

  7. 7
    Stolen Time Instrumental

    Moments taken when no one is watching, precious because they weren't supposed to last. Past the album's midpoint, it turns the passage of time into something tender and secret.

    More about this track

    A stripped-back acoustic country rock ballad, built on steady fingerpicked guitar and light percussion. Rolling thunder and soft rain fill the space around it and swell at each dramatic turn, so the quiet moments feel sheltered from the storm outside. That sense of a private pocket away from the world matches a title about time taken for yourself.

  8. 8
    Nightfall Instrumental

    The day finally gives way to dark. Arriving in the album's second half, it marks the hour the record has been drifting toward.

    More about this track

    Nightfall keeps to the same plain pairing of fingerpicked guitar and light percussion, set against rain and distant thunder. As each peak arrives, the storm rises with it, like dusk deepening into a stormy dark. The arrangement stays simple and close, letting the weather tell the listener that the day is over.

  9. 9
    Endless Moments Instrumental

    A wish for time to stop, set against an album full of time running out. Late in the record, it holds onto a feeling as if it could last forever.

    More about this track

    Syncopated fingerpicked guitar leads over minimal brushed percussion, with rolling thunder and soft rain weaving through the mix. The weather swells as the music rises, light electric blues licks join in the fuller passages, and muted synth pads add a floating, trance-like layer underneath. The steady patterns and drifting pads make time feel suspended, holding on to the moment the title wishes would last.

  10. 10
    Morning Dew Instrumental

    Dawn arrives out of order, after Nightfall rather than before it. Late in the album, the title brings fresh light and cool air into the long night.

    More about this track

    Morning Dew hears the same blend of fingerpicked guitar, brushed percussion and soft rain as the cool, damp hour just after a storm. Syncopated patterns move gently forward while rain and thunder drift through the mix, muted synth pads hang in the air like mist, and bluesy electric licks catch the light in the fuller sections. It feels like the world rinsed clean.

  11. 11
    Sun Down Instrumental

    The light leaves again, as if the album's day were turning on a loop. As the next-to-last title, it hands the story back to the dark.

    More about this track

    As the light goes, the fingerpicked guitar and brushed percussion pick up a dusky, bluesy edge, with electric licks joining in the fuller passages. Rolling thunder and rain rise with each swell, and muted ambient synths stretch out beneath them like a sky darkening toward night. It is the album's last look at daylight before the dark closes in.

  12. 12
    Midnight Rain Instrumental

    The album ends in the deepest part of the night, with rain falling. As the closing title, it leaves the turning day and the dying fire under one last soft storm.

    More about this track

    The closing track returns to the bare country ballad sound: steady fingerpicked guitar and light percussion moving at an unhurried pace. Soft rainfall and rolling thunder fill the dark around them and gather strength at each dramatic peak, as if the storm answers every rise in the song. Plain and close, it lets the weather have the last word on the album's long night.
